13. Course (Ryan: "spin up a tree map, and marble map, to do its best job making an obstacle course for both of those with the elements it created. no rules, just let them try their best"). The lane was empty and the bake found ONE cover point on the whole map (docs/BOT_SWEEP.md): every occluder stood in the outer wall, and a shadow from the outer wall falls away from the lane. 45 prop instances now stand ON the annulus, placed by `tools/forest_course.py` (the layout is the data in its `COURSE` table; `--check` proves the corridor, `--cover` predicts the bake) and spliced into `scenes/ring/forest.tscn` under `Sections/`. Nothing is welded into `forest.glb`.
    Two measured facts shape every placement, both off `tools/harness/check_course.gd`. **The walkable lane is 7.25 m, not 10.6 m**: the ferns and hummocks welded at r 46.7..47.6 and r 56.4..57.3 carry collision, so the baked mesh runs r 48.25..55.50. One prop band plus the 0.5 m agent radius eats half of that, so a prop stands INSIDE the lane (r 50.0) or OUTSIDE it (r 53.7) and never in the middle; the corridor is then the other side, never under 3.15 m. **The bake samples a body off the navmesh, which recast floats 0.5 m over the grass** (`--sight` reports `navmesh_y=23.50` on a lane at y 23.0), so a "head" is 2.0 m up and nothing under about 2.6 m takes the guard's head line. The 2.20 m bush and the 2.11 m slab are therefore scaled 1.35x and 1.4x in Y -- map 1's own lever on `rock_wall` (scale y 0.43) -- and the 1.30 m boulder and low bush are left as what they are, cover for a crouched body and not a standing one.
    The guard's eye is on the ring's axis, so a shadow falls radially OUTWARD: a prop inside the lane shades the corridor outside it, a prop outside the lane shades nothing a runner can stand in. That is the whole grammar of the lap. A stretch that puts its props inside is a covered run; a stretch that puts them outside is a naked one; the sides swap, and the swap is an open crossing.
